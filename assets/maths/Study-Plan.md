## Rules that make the order work

1. **Cover the solution, write yours, then mark.** Reading ladders is not practice; the "Markers want" line is the mark scheme.
2. **Do not skip A1 before A6.** Every Taylor question in the paper ultimately asks you to form a difference quotient or bound one.
3. **Do not cite anything outside the appendix by number.** Non-appendix results (Taylor, change of variables, integration by parts, linearity and additivity, monotone ⇒ integrable, the MVT for integrals, \(f'\equiv0\Rightarrow\) constant) must be *stated* before use — that is a mark you simply lose if you assume it.
4. **Bank the cheap marks first.** Questions 1–3 are worth **17 marks in Paper A** and **22 in Paper B**, and every theorem-statement part is 2–3 marks on its own — take them inside the first fifteen minutes.
5. **Every function comes with its domain; every theorem comes with its hypotheses checked on the correct closed or open interval.**
6. **Re-drill, don't re-read.** Step 5 exists because the memorandum tells you exactly which ladder to redo. Skipping that step is how the same mark is lost twice.

## What not to study

Lebl §5.5 (improper integrals), Chapter 6 (sequences of functions / uniform convergence), Chapter 7 (metric spaces), convexity, L'Hôpital's rule, and uniform differentiability do not appear in any Week 2–12 file or exercise solution and are treated as out of scope. Week 1 notes are absent from the folder; the one result they are cited for (sequential continuity, Lebl Prop 3.2.2) is covered in the teaching notes.
